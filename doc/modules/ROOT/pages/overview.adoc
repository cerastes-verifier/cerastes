= Overview

cerastes is a deductive verifier for C and pass:[C++], in development. You write
contracts in your source: what a function requires, what it ensures, what holds
at each turn of a loop. `cerastes verify` then proves that every function meets
its contract on every input the contract allows, or reports the line where it
cannot. This page says what cerastes is meant to be, what it does today and
where to start reading.

== Where to start

* *New to cerastes, or to verification:* the xref:tutorial/index.adoc[tutorial]
  builds the ideas one chapter at a time, from a first proof to pointers and the
  heap. Every program in it is checked by the verifier.
* *Looking up a rule:* the reference pages, starting with
  xref:verifying.adoc[Verifying a program] and xref:annotations.adoc[Annotations],
  say exactly what is accepted, what is checked and what every message means.
* *Building, testing or extending cerastes:* the
  xref:dev/index.adoc[developer guide].

== Design

You write contracts (preconditions, postconditions, loop invariants and
assertions) as `+[[cerastes::...]]+` attributes in your C or pass:[C++] source, usually
through the macros of `<cerastes.h>`, so that the same source still builds with
an ordinary compiler (xref:ordinary-compilers.adoc[Building with ordinary
compilers]). The EDG front end parses them as ordinary pass:[C++] expressions.
cerastes translates the program and its contracts into Viper, a verification
language. The Silicon verifier, which uses the Z3 solver, then checks them, and
cerastes reports every failure against a line of your C or pass:[C++] source, not
against generated code.

The distinguishing goal is to verify templates generically. A function
template is meant to be proved once, against the properties that its template
parameters are required to have, and the result is meant to hold for every
instantiation that meets those requirements. None of this works yet:
`cerastes verify` rejects function templates. The C language comes first.

== Pipeline

[source,text]
----
C/C++ source + contracts
        |
        v
  EDG front end            parses C23 and C++20 and the cerastes
        |                  annotations, in the cerastes configuration
        v
  Back end                 checks the rules EDG cannot check for the
        |                  annotations, rejects what it cannot verify,
        |                  and encodes the rest into a small internal IR
        v
  Viper text (.vpr)        written, with a map back to your source lines
        |
        v
  Silicon, Z3              prove each check or report it
        |
        v
  Errors at C/C++ lines    reported, with a category and a note
----

The xref:dev/architecture.adoc[architecture page] of the developer guide
describes each stage.

== What it verifies today

A verified function terminates and satisfies its contract, and nothing in it
overflows, divides by zero, reads a value it never wrote, or touches memory it
does not own. Today that covers, in C23 and in pass:[C++]20:

* *Integers and `bool`:* arithmetic in which nothing wraps, signed or unsigned,
  exact conversions, bitwise operators, enumerations and named constants
  (xref:integer-semantics.adoc[Integer semantics]).
* *Control flow:* `if`, `switch`, the loops with invariants and termination
  measures, `break`, `continue`, forward `goto`, recursion, functions that do
  not return, `assert` and `check`
  (xref:verifying.adoc[Verifying a program]).
* *Calls:* each call is checked against the callee's contract, and the caller
  knows only what that contract ensures. A function declared with a contract and
  defined elsewhere is trusted
  (xref:verifying.adoc#one-unit[the single-unit assumption]).
* *Memory:* pointers to local and global variables, pointer parameters and
  results with ownership contracts (`owns`, `reads`, `old`), pointers to
  pointers, C structures, local arrays (indexing checked against their bounds,
  pointers to their elements), contracts over ranges of elements
  (`cerastes_owns_range(p, n)`, `cerastes_reads_range(p, n)`), and single objects
  allocated with `malloc` and
  `calloc` and released with `free`, with leaks reported
  (xref:memory.adoc[Memory and ownership]).

Every message the tool prints is on xref:error-messages.adoc[Error messages].

Anything else is rejected with a diagnostic at its line, never approximated:
among it arrays outside a function's locals, statements about every element at
once (`forall`), pointer arithmetic, floating point, references, classes and
templates. The lists are on xref:verifying.adoc#not-supported[Verifying a
program] and xref:memory.adoc#not-yet[Memory and ownership]. Results are for
one translation unit.
